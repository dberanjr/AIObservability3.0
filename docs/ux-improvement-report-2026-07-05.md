## Summary page

_The Summary page is a dense, well-built navigation hub: a fleet-posture hero (radial trust gauge + three health-pillar bars + six KPI tiles with sparklines) over three customizable grids (Quality/cost/efficiency, Operations, Problem signals), every tile a live scoped KPI that drills into its owning tab. The engineering is strong (shared hooks, scan-cost attribution, sampling extrapolation), but as an executive front door it has real data-design weaknesses: the headline trust grade silently drops the quality pillar yet the copy still claims it; card-level query errors are swallowed so a broken query reads as "healthy / no issues"; the same scalar (hidden failures, grade, p95, spend) is repeated 2-3x, diluting hierarchy; and empty/loading states are inconsistent and cause-blind. Charts are mostly good, but a few encodings (sqrt-scaled pattern bars, amber-only deltas, denominator-less hidden-risk count) understate severity._

**Summary-1. ⭐ Dedupe the same scalar repeated across hero and cards**  ·  _high impact · S effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: The hidden-failure count appears three times on one screen: hero KPI 'Hidden risk · 200-OK failures' (PostureBand), the 'Behind 200' MiniTile in QualityTrustCard, and the whole 'Hidden · 200-OK' ops card. The trust grade+index appears twice (hero gauge + QualityTrustCard headerRight 'B · 78'). Fleet P95 appears twice (hero KPI + LatencyTierCard center). 30-day spend appears twice (hero footer '30-day spend' + KPI 'Spend · 30d proj'). This repetition flattens visual hierarchy and spends the executive's limited attention on the same three numbers.
- Fix: Assign each scalar one home: hero owns the six headline KPIs + grade; cards own the decomposition only. Drop the 'Behind 200' MiniTile and the duplicate grade chip from QualityTrustCard (it already sits under the hero gauge), remove the redundant hero-footer '30-day spend' line now that a Spend KPI tile exists, and let the Latency card lead with tier mix rather than re-printing P95.

**Summary-2. ⭐ Stop the trust grade from silently excluding the quality pillar**  ·  _high impact · M effort · insight_
- Problem: compositeTrust() (posture.ts) renormalizes over only the pillars that carry data, and on this tenant the quality/eval pillar is routinely 'no-data' (usePulseHealth.qualityPillar returns null score when gen_ai.evaluation.* is absent). So the hero gauge can show a confident 'A · 92' computed from just operational+cost, with the 0.35-weight quality dimension entirely missing — and useFleetPosture's headline still says 'Healthy across operational, quality, and cost.' For an AI-observability exec page, quietly grading trust while omitting the trust/quality signal is the most misleading thing on the screen.
- Fix: When the quality pillar is no-data: (1) suppress 'quality' from the headline string (list only the pillars actually scored, or append 'quality unmeasured'); (2) add a small coverage caption under the gauge like 'grade from 2 of 3 pillars' and render the missing quality pillar bar in a visibly 'not measured' style (dashed track + 'not measured' instead of '—' at 0%); (3) consider capping the letter grade or badging it when a high-weight pillar is absent so an 'A' can't imply verified quality.

**Summary-3. ⭐ Surface card-level query errors instead of swallowing them into empty states**  ·  _high impact · M effort · empty-states_
- Problem: SummaryPage computes firstError = summary.error ?? posture.error only. Every other card hook (useHiddenFailures, useModels/useModelConcentration, useFinOps, useAgentCosts, useLatencyDecomposition, useProblemPatternCounts, useAnomalies, useDailySpend, useAgentEval, useTokenEfficiency, useProviderMix) can error, and those errors are never surfaced — the card falls through to its 'No X in scope' one-liner. A failed Problem-patterns or Top-findings query therefore renders as 'No issues detected', i.e. reassuring good news. The single ErrorBanner is also rendered with no onRetry, so even the surfaced error can't be retried.
- Fix: Give each SummaryCard a distinct error branch (reuse ErrorState bare) that is visually different from the empty state, driven by that card's own hook.error; or aggregate all card errors into the top banner with a per-source label. Pass an onRetry (refetch) into ErrorBanner so it renders its Retry button. Never let an errored query render as 'No issues / No failures'.

**Summary-4. ⭐ Gate the drag/resize/collapse customization behind an explicit Edit-layout mode**  ·  _high impact · M effort · interaction_
- Problem: The page ships four independent CustomizableGrids (kpis, quality, operations, signals), each tile carrying a drag grip strip, a bottom-right resize handle, a persisted layout, and a floating '⟲ Reset layout' button, plus a per-tile collapse chevron (CollapsibleTile). That is a very heavy, low-discoverability interaction surface (grip strips + 12px resize glyphs) for an executive landing page whose job is to be read in five seconds, and the orientation hint only appears on the first section.
- Fix: Default to a read-only layout and put all reorder/resize/reset affordances behind a single 'Customize' / 'Edit layout' toggle in the page header. Keep collapse always-available (it's cheap and useful), but hide drag strips and resize handles until edit mode is on, so the default first impression is calm and the customization is opt-in for power users.

**Summary-5. Fix the mixed time-bases inside the 'Spend · 30d proj' KPI tile**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · insight_
- Problem: The kpi-spend tile (PostureBand) shows three different windows at once: value = daily.projected30d (an 8-day-derived 30-day projection from useDailySpend), delta = daily.delta24h (day-over-day), and spark = summary.spark.spend (per-bucket spend across the global timeframe, which could be the last 1h). So the headline is a monthly projection, the trend arrow is a 1-day change, and the sparkline is intra-timeframe buckets — three unrelated bases stacked as if they describe one thing. It's also the only rate/value KPI with no average reference line.
- Fix: Make the tile internally consistent: either show a daily-spend series (useDailySpend.bars, last 7-8 days) whose last point matches the projection basis and whose delta is day-over-day, or relabel explicitly (value 'proj. 30d', spark caption 'spend, current timeframe', delta 'd/d'). Add the same mean() reference line the cost/req, p95 and error tiles use.

**Summary-6. Give the hidden-risk number a denominator and its composition bar a legend**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · insight_
- Problem: The hero 'Hidden risk' KPI shows a raw extrapolated count ('1,240 · 200-OK failures') with no base rate — an exec can't tell if that's 0.1% or 20% of responses. Its footer CompositionBar (PostureBand) encodes refusals / truncation / content-filter / other purely by color with the only labels in a single container title tooltip; there is no inline legend, so the four-color bar is undecipherable at a glance (unlike the HiddenFailuresCard, which uses a labeled BarList).
- Fix: Show hidden failures as both count and rate ('1,240 · 3.1% of LLM responses') by dividing by the LLM-span population already available via the summary/requests. Add a compact inline legend (2-3 colored dot + label chips) beneath the CompositionBar, or at minimum per-segment titles, so the split is readable without hovering the whole bar.

**Summary-7. Use linear (not sqrt) scaling for the 'ranked by match volume' pattern bars**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · dataviz_
- Problem: ProblemPatternsCard/PatternRow computes bar width as Math.sqrt(count)/Math.sqrt(max)*100. On a list whose stated purpose is 'ranked by match volume', sqrt compression makes a detector with 9 matches (30% width) look nearly as loud as one with 100 (100% width becomes the only differentiator), visually understating the dominant pattern the exec should act on first. The count text is shown, so the number is recoverable, but the primary visual encoding contradicts the ranking intent.
- Fix: Switch the bar fill to linear (count/max). If a few high-volume detectors would otherwise flatten the small ones, use log scaling with an explicit note, but don't use sqrt silently on a volume-ranked list. Keep the same-span/cross-span color coding.

**Summary-8. Add a severity tier to the KPI delta colors**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · dataviz_
- Problem: deltaLabel (PostureBand) colors any 'bad' movement with a single --amber and reserves --green-2 for 'good', never using --red and never scaling by magnitude. So an error-rate KPI trending +400% shows the same mild amber as +3%, and the hero can't distinguish a routine wobble from a regression worth paging on. This also clashes with the pillar bars, which do use --red for critical — two different severity ramps in the same hero.
- Fix: Introduce a magnitude threshold in deltaLabel (e.g. |pct| over a per-metric bound → --red, moderate → --amber, good → --green-2) so severe regressions read as red and align with the pillar ramp. Keep the arrow glyph as the non-color cue for accessibility.

**Summary-9. Standardize empty states and distinguish no-data causes**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · empty-states_
- Problem: Only QualityTrustCard uses the rich EmptyState (title + description + CTA to emit gen_ai.evaluation.*). Every other card falls back to a bare gray one-liner: 'No latency data in scope.', 'No provider activity in scope.', 'No priced model spend...', 'No attributed agent cost...', 'No hidden 200-OK failures...', 'No issues detected...'. These can't distinguish 'no AI instrumented' vs 'scope filter excludes everything' vs genuinely zero, and when canQueryScope is false (no scope resolved yet) the enabled:false query leaves isLoading false + data undefined, so cards render 'No X in scope' even though nothing was queried. 'No issues detected' / 'No hidden failures' also read as good news rather than possibly-absent instrumentation.
- Fix: Route every card's empty branch through EmptyState with cause-specific copy, and add a shared distinct 'Select a scope to see data' state keyed off canQueryScope (vs a real zero result). For safety-framed tiles (Top findings, Hidden failures, Problem patterns) word the true-zero case as 'No signals in this window' with a hint that it depends on instrumentation, so absence never masquerades as an all-clear.

**Summary-10. Unify loading idioms across the page**  ·  _low impact · S effort · consistency_
- Problem: The page mixes three loading languages: the hero shows bare '…' ellipses (grade, hidden total, 30-day spend) and a 'Scoring pillars…' text line, while the cards below show Skeleton blocks (FinOps, Latency, Hidden, Agents, Activity, Findings, Patterns). Side by side, the ellipsis tiles read as 'broken/tiny' next to neat skeletons, and there's no consistent 'this is loading' signal in the hero.
- Fix: Pick one idiom: give the hero gauge, KPI values, and pillar rows Skeleton placeholders (matching the cards) during posture.isLoading/daily.isLoading, and drop the ad-hoc '…' and 'Scoring pillars…' strings. Reserve the ellipsis only for sub-second in-place refreshes if at all.

**Summary-11. Label the per-tile time window where tiles ignore the global timeframe**  ·  _low impact · S effort · labeling_
- Problem: The page comment and most tiles follow the global scope.timeframe, but several are fixed-window: 'Activity · 24h' (useActivityHistogram) is always 24h, and the hero Spend value/daily bars come from useDailySpend's own 8-day scan regardless of the toolbar timeframe. When a user narrows the global timeframe to, say, 1h, those tiles keep showing 24h/30d numbers with no indication, so the page silently mixes windows.
- Fix: Add a small window chip to each tile header that states its actual basis ('24h', '30d proj', 'current timeframe') so mixed windows are explicit, and make Activity honor the global timeframe (or clearly mark it as a fixed 24h overview) so 'the global timeframe applies everywhere' actually holds.

**Summary-12. Add accessible labels to the radial gauges and clarify the efficiency tile's two numbers**  ·  _low impact · M effort · a11y_
- Problem: MiniPartialDonut renders its svg aria-hidden and conveys the trust index and token-efficiency scores only through visually-centered spans with no programmatic label tying value to meaning; a screen reader hears '92 88 trust index' with no role/label. In EfficiencyMixCard the gauge encodes eff.score (a 0-100 composite) while the adjacent big number is outputPerDollar (a raw count like '12,450 output tokens per dollar') — two unrelated scales side by side that a reader can easily conflate.
- Fix: Wrap each gauge in a labeled element (role='img' + aria-label like 'Fleet trust index 92 of 100, grade A') so the value and its meaning are announced together. In the efficiency tile, either drop the score gauge in favor of the single outputPerDollar figure with a trend, or explicitly caption the gauge ('efficiency score /100') so the two numbers read as distinct metrics.


## Pulse

_The hero is an interactive 8-tier node-map (ArchitectureMap → NodeMap/MapNode) wired to real gen_ai.* spans via useArchitectureData, with a lens system, per-tier drawers, a fleet loop arc, edge-volume weighting, and a 13-tile summary side panel. The data model is genuinely rich and honest about telemetry gaps (ghost/inferred tiers), but the presentation leans heavily on hover-only reveals, a tiny red/amber/green dot as the sole health cue, and a lens interaction that silently drops the tier numbers. The biggest weaknesses for a page titled "live health": the map has no at-rest health verdict, the "Live" pulse is always green regardless of staleness, and the data-query error path (useArchitectureData returns `error`) is never surfaced inside the map — it just shimmers forever on failure._

**Pulse-1. ⭐ Surface the map's own data-load error instead of shimmering forever**  ·  _high impact · S effort · empty-states_
- Problem: useArchitectureData returns an `error` field (ArchData.error, line ~710), but ArchitectureMap only branches on `data.empty` / `data.isLoading` — it never reads `data.error`. PulsePage only renders `summary.error` from usePulseSummary. If the archMap summarize/timeseries query fails (scan-limit, DQL, auth), the tiles stay in permanent shimmer / '—' with no message on a page whose whole job is live health.
- Fix: In ArchitectureMap, when `data.error` is set, replace the NodeMap body with an inline error state (reuse ErrorBanner or a compact retry card) that names the failure and offers retry, rather than falling through to the shimmer/empty branches.

**Pulse-2. ⭐ Make the 'Live' indicator reflect staleness instead of always pulsing green**  ·  _high impact · S effort · labeling_
- Problem: am-live-dot is hard-coded to var(--am-health) and the am-ping animation always runs, while relTime(data.refreshedMs, now) can read '2h ago'. Data staleTime is 60s and `now` ticks every 30s, so 'Live' with a green pulsing dot overstates freshness and stays green even when refreshedMs is stale or null ('refreshing…'). On a live-health page this is a misleading signal.
- Fix: Drive the dot color and the ping off recency: green+ping when refreshedMs is within ~1 refresh interval, amber/no-ping when stale, grey when null. Consider relabeling 'Live' to 'Auto-refresh · {relTime}' so the 60s-scoped-DQL cadence isn't read as real-time streaming.

**Pulse-3. ⭐ Make the lens banner actionable and fix its info-severity miscolor**  ·  _high impact · S effort · consistency_
- Problem: computeBanner maps `top.severity === 'info' ? 'warning' : top.severity`, so an informational finding renders with amber warning styling (am-banner[data-status=warning] + amber dot) — overstating severity. The banner also shows only the single top finding with no '+N more on this path' and is not clickable, while mapCss defines am-banner-cta / am-banner-savings / am-banner-action styles that are never rendered (dead CSS) — the intended drill CTA is missing.
- Fix: Map 'info' to a neutral/info tone (not warning). Make the banner headline open the finding's DetailModal (openSpec {kind:'finding'}), append a '+N more findings on this path' count from the on-path filter, and wire the existing am-banner-cta style to a real drill button.

**Pulse-4. ⭐ Add an at-rest overall-health verdict above the map**  ·  _high impact · M effort · insight_
- Problem: At rest (no lens) there is no roll-up of system health — the banner (computeBanner) only renders when a lens is active. The user must visually scan 8 tiles' tiny status dots plus the 'N findings' chip to answer 'is anything wrong?'. The page is literally the live-health surface yet gives no top-line answer, and the worst-tier status computed per node (worse()/rateStatus) is never aggregated.
- Fix: Compute the worst tier status + finding breakdown already available in useArchitectureData (nodes[*].status, breakdown.critical/warning/info) into a one-line verdict rendered in the header at rest, e.g. 'Healthy' / 'Degraded — LLM tier warning · 2 critical findings', color-coded, clickable to the findings scope modal. This anchors visual hierarchy on the primary question.

**Pulse-5. ⭐ Fix the lens interaction: keep tier numbers and add an explicit Overview/reset**  ·  _high impact · M effort · interaction_
- Problem: Two issues. (1) When a lens is active but a tier has no cell for it, resolveCell (model.ts) returns a muted cell with NO headline, so MapNode falls to `am-node-sub` prose — e.g. under the Cost lens the Agent/Tools/Memory tiles show only cost-driver sentences ('scratchpad re-sent as input tokens') with no number, making the map text-heavy and asymmetric. (2) There is no 'Overview' pill; the only way back to the default view is to click the already-active pill again (setLensId toggle), which is undiscoverable.
- Fix: For tiers without a lens cell, keep the base headline (dimmed) with the lens caption as a sub-line instead of dropping the number. Add a leading 'Overview' pill (default-active) so the toggle model is visible, or render a clear reset affordance next to the 'Lens' label.

**Pulse-6. ⭐ Don't encode tier health with only a tiny red/amber/green dot**  ·  _high impact · M effort · a11y_
- Problem: Per-tier health is conveyed almost entirely by an 8px am-node-dot at the corner of the icon plus the headline number turning amber/red. The card's border-top color encodes CATEGORY (indigo core / teal resource), not health, so a critical tier still has a category-colored top border. The sole health channel is the classic RAG trio with no redundant shape/text — a problem for color-blind users and quick scanning.
- Fix: Add a redundant, larger status cue on non-healthy tiers: a status ring/left-accent bar, a small glyph (▲ warning / ● critical), or a 'warning'/'critical' text tag, so status is legible without relying on the dot's hue. Keep the category accent separate from status.

**Pulse-7. ⭐ Stop hiding the map's core quantitative data behind hover**  ·  _high impact · M effort · interaction_
- Problem: The map's main quantitative payload — per-edge call volume (am-edge-rate) and sparkline point values — only appears on mouse hover (hoverEdge in NodeMap, onMove in Spark). The legend claims 'edge = call volume' but no numbers show at rest, and none of this is reachable on touch/trackpad. Users can't compare tier throughput without hovering each edge one at a time.
- Fix: Render always-on rate labels on at least the highest-volume edges (data.edgeRate is already computed), and show the latest sparkline value as a static end-label on each node card. Keep hover for the full per-bucket readout.

**Pulse-8. Rescale edge thickness so mid-volume edges stay distinguishable**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · dataviz_
- Problem: edgeWeight = max(EDGE_FLOOR 0.12, count[e.to] / maxCount) with maxCount = the single busiest tier (useArchitectureData). Because LLM calls typically dwarf retrievals/memory ops, most edges collapse to the 0.12 floor and render at ~4.5px (wpx = 2.5 + w*17) — visually identical thin lines — so the 'edge = call volume' encoding conveys almost nothing except on the one dominant edge. There is also no scale reference in the legend.
- Fix: Apply a perceptual scale (sqrt or log of the ratio) before mapping to px so a 10x-smaller edge still reads as clearly thinner rather than bottoming out at the floor, and add a small 'thin→thick' scale hint to the legend.

**Pulse-9. Collapse the tier drawer's stack of five charts and hide flat-zero series**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · dataviz_
- Problem: NodeDrawer renders up to five stacked DrawerCharts (Throughput, Latency, Errors, Tokens, Spend), each only suppressed when it has <2 points — an all-zero series (e.g. a tier with no errors) still draws a flat red 'Errors' line. This is a long vertical scroll of mostly low-signal small charts, and the color choices (throughput=status color, latency=blue, errors=red, tokens=purple, spend=green) aren't keyed to any legend.
- Fix: Skip series that are entirely zero, and either combine throughput/latency/errors into one dual-axis chart with a shared time axis and a small legend, or use compact small-multiples so the drawer reads as one coherent chart block instead of five separate ones.

**Pulse-10. Reduce the LLM tile's badge soup and prioritize anomalies**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: The LLM node can carry five badges (≈$spend, N tok, p90, X% truncated, 429s — useArchitectureData baseBadges) at 10px font inside a 230px card, wrapping into a dense pill cluster. The 'err' badge on Agent/Tools duplicates the status already signalled by the headline color, adding redundancy and cognitive load to the busiest tier.
- Fix: Cap on-card badges to the 2 most important plus any anomalous ones (truncated/429s), move the rest (raw tokens, p90) into the drawer, and drop the plain 'err%' pill when the headline color already conveys error status.

**Pulse-11. Give the whole-map empty state a preserved diagram and real actions**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · empty-states_
- Problem: When data.empty && !isLoading, the entire NodeMap is replaced by a single grey sentence ('No AI spans in the current scope / scan budget — widen the timeframe or raise the scan limit.'). This discards the teaching value of the architecture diagram and only names remedies as prose — 'widen the timeframe' and 'raise the scan limit' are not clickable, even though scope/sampling controls exist elsewhere in the app.
- Fix: Keep the ghosted skeleton diagram visible and overlay a compact empty card with actual action buttons (Widen timeframe, Raise scan limit) that invoke the existing scope/sampling setters, so the empty state teaches the model and offers a one-click fix.

**Pulse-12. Tame the DataGapNote wall of text and its instrumentation jargon**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · labeling_
- Problem: Directly under the hero, DataGapNote renders a long dense paragraph plus best-practice text full of internal codes (P1.2, P1.5, P1.1, P0.1, 'INSTRUMENTATION-REQUIREMENTS.md') and four raw attribute names. It's high-value context but reads as a jargon wall to anyone who isn't the instrumentation owner, and its prominence competes with the map for attention.
- Fix: Show a one-line summary ('Quality scoring, TTFT, and per-user spend are unavailable — telemetry gaps') with an expandable 'What's missing / how to fix' section, and translate the P-codes into plain requirement names on hover so the gap is understandable without the requirements doc.


## Explorer page

_The Explorer is a well-instrumented catalog: 7 summary tiles, heuristic findings, a service×model token heatmap with a rich cost/pricing/golden-signals modal, a RAG panel, and an AI-services table — all driven off a single gen_ai spans query with URL-backed hybrid filters. Its biggest weaknesses are (1) three data surfaces (heatmap, RAG panel, detail modal) silently render query errors as "no data" empty states, misleading users during outages; (2) the heatmap's dataviz mixes two encodings (hue=provider, saturation=tokens) with no legend and silently truncates to 30 rows while claiming the full count; and (3) the primary AI-services table can't be sorted and omits the one metric this FinOps tool is built to surface — estimated cost — even though the pricing math already exists. Several tile/labeling/a11y details add avoidable cognitive load._

**Explorer-1. ⭐ Render query errors instead of masking them as empty states**  ·  _high impact · S effort · empty-states_
- Problem: In ServiceModelHeatmapBody the hook returns result.error but the JSX only branches on isLoading → rows.length===0 → 'No usage data in the current scope.' A failed heatmap DQL query therefore looks identical to a genuinely empty scope. RagPanelBody has the same bug (useRag returns error, never rendered → shows an empty BarList), and useServiceModelDetail doesn't even return error, so a failed detail query renders 'No data for this service / model pair.' ExplorerPage's comment claims heatmap errors 'surface within that card', but no code renders them.
- Fix: Add an error branch above the empty branch in ServiceModelHeatmapBody and RagPanelBody (reuse the existing ErrorBanner/ErrorState component already imported in ExplorerPage), and have useServiceModelDetail return `error` so ServiceModelModal can show an error row instead of the 'No data for this pair' text. Distinguish 'query failed' (retry affordance) from 'zero rows' (genuine empty) in all three.

**Explorer-2. ⭐ Add an estimated-cost column to the AI services table**  ·  _high impact · M effort · insight_
- Problem: AIServicesTable surfaces requests, tokens, tok/req, agents, errors and logical errors, but never cost — despite serviceModelCost.ts + data/pricing already computing USD, and the heatmap-cell modal showing a full cost trio. For a FinOps-oriented AI-observability tool, dollars are the headline metric, yet a user must open the heatmap and click each cell to see any cost. The Concentration tile even frames the story around 'fleet cost' with no cost figure anywhere in the main table.
- Fix: Compute a per-service estimated cost from each row's tokens × model pricing (aggregate costOf across the row's models) and add a right-aligned '$ (est.)' column with the same ≈estimated badge convention the modal uses when pricing is blended/Unknown. Sort default by cost desc, or offer it as a sort key.

**Explorer-3. ⭐ Make the AI services table sortable and expose row-count truncation**  ·  _high impact · M effort · interaction_
- Problem: The table is a hand-rolled flex grid whose order is fixed to the DQL `sort tokens desc | limit 200`; headers (Service, LLM req, Tokens, Errors, Logical err, Tok/req…) are plain text with no click-to-sort, so an operator hunting the worst error rate or highest tok/req can't reorder. There's also no indication when 200 services is hit, and the container uses role='row' cells without table/columnheader semantics.
- Fix: Make each numeric header a sort toggle (client-side over aiServices.filtered, which is already in memory) with an aria-sort indicator, and show a 'showing 200 of N — narrow the scope' footer when the query hits the limit. Consider adopting Strato's DataTable for built-in sort + a11y roles rather than the bespoke flex table.

**Explorer-4. ⭐ Give the heatmap a color legend and stop conflating provider hue with token magnitude**  ·  _high impact · M effort · dataviz_
- Problem: cellColor() log-scales saturation of the provider's hue per column (color-mix at 5–90%), so magnitude is encoded as saturation while hue encodes provider. A dark Anthropic cell and a dark OpenAI cell are different hues at the same intensity, defeating cross-column magnitude comparison — the whole point of a heatmap — and there is no legend at all, only the subtitle text 'log-scaled cell color'. Users must hover every cell to read a value.
- Fix: Use a single sequential scale for magnitude across the whole grid (keep provider identity in the existing column-header dot only), and add a small gradient legend with anchored values (e.g. min / median / max tokens, noting the log scale). If per-provider hue must stay, add a per-column legend so intensities are only compared within a column.

**Explorer-5. Fix the heatmap header count vs the 30-row render cap**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · labeling_
- Problem: The header prints `{result.rows.length} services · {result.columns.length} models`, but the body renders `result.rows.slice(0, 30)`. With 50 services the header says 50 while only 30 rows appear, with no 'top 30' note — a silent data-integrity/labeling gap. Columns are uncapped (query limit 1000), so many models produce an unbounded horizontal scroll with no virtualization.
- Fix: Either render all rows or change the header to 'Top 30 of N services (by tokens)' and add a subtle 'N more not shown' row. Cap or lazy-reveal columns similarly, and confirm the sticky service column stays legible as the grid widens.

**Explorer-6. Make the Errors tile convey rate and severity, not just an absolute amber count**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · dataviz_
- Problem: The Errors tile shows fmtCount(summary.errors) and goes amber whenever errors>0 — so 1 failed span and 10,000 failed spans look equally 'amber', and a large absolute count with a tiny rate (e.g. 10 errors in 10M requests) reads as alarming. The per-service table already shows 'N (rate%)', but the fleet tile drops the denominator entirely, and never escalates to red.
- Fix: Show the fleet error rate as the value (or as the sub-line), e.g. '0.4% · 12 errors', and drive emphasis off the rate: default <1%, amber 1–5%, red >5% — mirroring the AIServicesTable status-dot thresholds so the tile and rows agree.

**Explorer-7. Clarify the cryptic 'Concentration' tile and add tile tooltips**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · labeling_

## Attributes page

_The page audits ~110 OpenTelemetry/OpenLLMetry/MCP attributes across 10 category sections, running one scoped DQL count-query per section (useAttributeAudit.ts) and marking each attribute present/missing at >=1 span. It renders a hero coverage ring + per-tier KPIs (AttributeAuditPage.tsx), a clickable TOC, grouped SectionCards with an attribute-cell grid, and a detail modal. The biggest weaknesses are all in data framing: the headline coverage ring counts Tier D anti-patterns and deprecated spellings in its denominator/numerator (so emitting a PII anti-pattern raises your score), aggregate numbers settle to misleading partial values during the staggered 10-query load, per-section query errors are silently rendered as "no spans," and the default view dumps ~110 cells (all tiers, all sections expanded) with no gaps-first / missing-only path to the one thing that matters — which mandatory attributes are missing and where._

**Attributes-1. ⭐ Default to an actionable subset instead of the full ~110-cell wall**  ·  _high impact · S effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: Every section renders expanded by default and every tier (including Tier D noise/anti-patterns) is shown, so users land on a dense grid dominated by opt-in and deprecated attributes they mostly cannot act on. Section 1 (LLM) alone renders ~50+ cells. This buries the mandatory/important signal under noise and makes the page feel like a spec dump rather than an audit.
- Fix: Default activeTiers to new Set(['A','B']) (the tiers the page itself calls Mandatory + Important), and let users opt into 'Nice to Have' and 'Other' explicitly. Consider defaulting sections below the first pillar to collapsed. Keep the existing 'Show all' reset. This immediately cuts first-paint density roughly in half and foregrounds the attributes that carry action.

**Attributes-2. ⭐ Fix misleading aggregates during the staggered 10-query load**  ·  _high impact · S effort · empty-states_
- Problem: 10 sections each call useScopedDql independently and resolve at different times. The hero skeleton guard is `audit.isLoading && overview.presentTotal === 0`, so the moment the FIRST section returns any present attr, the ring and all four tier KPIs flip from em-dash to real-looking values and then keep climbing as the other 9 queries land. There is no cue that the number is still assembling, so a user reading the ring at t+400ms sees a settled-looking but wrong coverage %. The TOC bars fill in the same piecemeal way.
- Fix: Gate the aggregate ring + tier KPIs on audit.isLoading (all sections done), not on presentTotal===0. While loading, keep the skeleton or overlay a lightweight 'X of 10 categories loaded' progress indicator (results.filter(r=>!r.isLoading).length) so the climbing numbers are legible as in-progress rather than final.

**Attributes-3. ⭐ Stop rendering per-section query errors as 'No spans'**  ·  _high impact · S effort · empty-states_
- Problem: In useAttributeAudit, a failed section query yields res.data===undefined, so rawSpans===0 and noData becomes true. SectionCard destructures noData but not error (SectionResult.error is computed and then ignored), so a broken query renders the reassuring gray message 'No spans in this section's population… Widen the timeframe or clear filters.' That tells the user their data is clean when the query actually failed. Only the first error surfaces globally via ErrorBanner; sections 2–10 fail silently.
- Fix: Pass result.error into SectionCard and branch on it before noData: show an inline error affordance ('Couldn't evaluate this category — retry') distinct from the legitimate empty state. This also prevents a failed section from inflating overview.sectionsEmpty vs. being counted as a real gap.

**Attributes-4. ⭐ Make the headline ring measure Mandatory coverage, not all-tier coverage**  ·  _high impact · M effort · dataviz_
- Problem: overview.coveragePct = presentTotal / TOTAL_ATTRIBUTES counts every tier, including Tier D. Tier D attributes are anti-patterns/deprecated spellings you WANT missing (e.g. gen_ai.input.messages content capture = 'PII risk', gen_ai.prompt.0.content, vector_db.query.text). So a workload that emits raw prompt content or legacy spellings scores HIGHER on the hero ring, and a correctly-suppressed one scores lower. Worse, because dozens of opt-in C/D attributes are structurally rare, all-tier coverage can almost never approach the coverageTone 80% green threshold, so a well-instrumented fleet reads perpetual amber/red. The CoverageRing is the largest, most-read element on the page and it rewards the wrong behavior.
- Fix: Drive CoverageRing from overview.mandatoryCoveragePct (Tier A) — the number that is actionable and can legitimately reach 100%. Keep all-tier as a small secondary caption ('X% of all A–D attributes seen'). Exclude Tier D from any 'coverage %' numerator entirely; for D, present is the negative signal. Apply the same fix to SectionOverview's per-row tone/bar (currently present===total can never go green for any section containing rarely-present D attributes) by basing the TOC bar on A+B coverage.

**Attributes-5. ⭐ Add a 'gaps-first' path — surface which mandatory attributes are missing and where**  ·  _high impact · M effort · insight_
- Problem: The core job of an audit is 'what am I missing?', but nothing supports it. Default activeTiers is A–D and default collapsed={} (all expanded), so the first paint is ~110 attribute cells in catalog order with present and missing interleaved. The hero says 'Mandatory 7/9' but never says WHICH 2 are missing or in which of the 10 sections — you must eyeball every card. There is no missing-only filter and no sort.

Add a top-of-page callout listing missing Tier A attributes with a jump link to their section (you already have jumpToSection + overview.tierStats.A). Optionally sort AttrCells missing-first within each grid so gaps rise to the top of the wall.

**Attributes-6. ⭐ Distinguish 'present but sparse' from 'genuinely present'**  ·  _high impact · M effort · insight_

The per-attr share bar hints at this, but the section 'present/total' KPI, the tier stats, and the hero ring all treat a 1-span attribute identically to a 100%-coverage attribute — manufacturing false confidence, exactly the failure mode the page's own sampling caveat warns about.

## Accessibility sweep

_The app is visually polished and does several a11y things right (segmented controls use role=radiogroup/radio, legend swatches are aria-hidden with real text labels beside them, status dots pair color with a text label in AgentsTable, FilterTrigger and heatmap cells are keyboard-operable with Enter/Space). But the accessibility layer is skin-deep in three high-impact ways: (1) every modal declares role=dialog/aria-modal yet does no real focus management — no focus-in, no focus trap, no focus restore, no background inerting; (2) the ~67 custom `all: unset` buttons have no visible focus ring because only four CSS classes define :focus-visible; and (3) the "Color-blindness filter" is actually a simulator that degrades color for real colorblind users while being presented as an accommodation. Secondary issues: low-contrast --text-3/--text-4 on small captions, color-only saturation encoding in the Explorer heatmap with no scale legend, and a hardcoded/uninformative aria-label on the reusable Donut._

**Accessibility-1. ⭐ Give all:unset buttons a global :focus-visible ring**  ·  _high impact · S effort · a11y_
- Problem: 67 buttons across pages/components/layout/pricing/tweaks use `all: unset`, which strips the native focus outline, but theme/tokens.ts defines :focus-visible outlines for only four classes (aaa-attr-cell, aaa-toc-row, aiobs-filter-trigger, aiobs-clickable-tile). So the modal close (×) buttons, the Tweaks accent swatches and segmented radios, the green 'Filter to this service + model' action (ServiceModelModal.tsx:355), the Sampling/ScanLimit/ModelType segmented toggles, and collapsible headers show nothing at all when reached by keyboard — a WCAG 2.4.7 failure across most of the custom UI.
- Fix: Add a single fallback rule to themeCss: `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--blue); outline-offset: 1px; border-radius: inherit; }`, then verify the segmented radios and swatch buttons pick it up (they set all:unset so they will). This one rule fixes the majority of the 67 controls without touching each call site.

**Accessibility-2. ⭐ Add real focus management to every modal**  ·  _high impact · M effort · a11y_
- Problem: DetailModalShell (components/DetailModal.tsx:140), ServiceModelModal (Explorer/ServiceModelModal.tsx:182), ChartModal (charts/ChartExpander.tsx:83), TweaksPanel (tweaks/TweaksPanel.tsx:357), ModelPricingPanel (pricing/ModelPricingPanel.tsx:646) and archMap/DetailModal.tsx:28 all set role=dialog + aria-modal but only wire Esc + backdrop click. None of them move focus into the dialog on open, none trap Tab, none restore focus to the trigger on close, and the page behind the scrim is never inert/aria-hidden. Open a Models row or Explorer heatmap-cell modal and press Tab: focus walks onto the page controls behind the overlay, and screen readers read the whole background despite aria-modal.
- Fix: Create one shared useModalA11y(ref, onClose) hook and use it in every dialog shell: on mount capture document.activeElement, move focus to the dialog container (or the close button), trap Tab/Shift+Tab within the dialog's focusable set, set aria-hidden/inert on sibling roots while open, and restore focus to the saved element on unmount. Retrofit the six dialogs above so the behavior is identical everywhere.

**Accessibility-3. ⭐ Reframe or fix the colorblind 'filter' — today it harms colorblind users**  ·  _high impact · M effort · a11y_
- Problem: ColorBlindFilters.tsx defines feColorMatrix values that SIMULATE protanopia/deuteranopia/tritanopia/achromatopsia, and TweaksContext.tsx:200 applies them to document.body. TweaksPanel.tsx:479 files this under an 'Accessibility' heading as 'Color-blindness filter' with options off/protanopia/…/grayscale. Simulation is a design-QA aid; applied for an actual colorblind user it stacks a second color deficiency on top of theirs and makes charts less distinguishable, not more. It is mislabeled as an accommodation.
- Fix: Either relabel and move it out of 'Accessibility' — e.g. a 'Simulate color vision (design preview)' toggle with helper text saying it previews how charts look to colorblind users — or replace the simulation matrices with daltonization/correction matrices that redistribute confusable hues so it genuinely helps. Pick one; don't ship a simulator badged as an accommodation.

**Accessibility-4. Restore a focus indicator on the InfoTooltip icon**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · a11y_
- Problem: InfoTooltip.tsx renders a focusable 'i' icon (tabIndex=0, role=img, aria-label=text) but hardcodes `outline: 'none'` at line 116. Keyboard users tabbing across the many metric/panel help icons get no visible focus state on the icon itself (the popover does open on focus, but the icon gives no cue it is focused), so they can't tell where they are.
- Fix: Remove `outline: 'none'` and instead add a :focus-visible style (e.g. `outline: 2px solid var(--blue); outline-offset: 2px`) via a className, so focus is visible while keeping the mouse experience clean.

**Accessibility-5. Raise contrast of --text-4 (and small --text-3 captions)**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · a11y_
- Problem: In theme/tokens.ts, --text-4 is #a4a29a (light, ~2.6:1 on #ffffff) and #5c5b56 (dark, ~2.7:1 on #131316) — below even the 3:1 non-text threshold — yet it carries meaningful content such as the AgentsTable no-data '—' (AgentsTable.tsx ~line 511) and the InfoTooltip border. --text-3 (~4.6–4.7:1) is borderline and is used for many 10–11px captions where 4.5:1 is required, and the amber 'estimated rate' badge (#B45F06) drops to ~4.0:1 on the dark surface at 10px uppercase.
- Fix: Darken --text-4 in light theme and lighten it in dark theme to clear 3:1, and reserve it strictly for decorative pixels (grips, hairlines) — route the '—' no-data marker to --text-3. Nudge --text-3 up to ≥4.5:1 for the small-caption use, or promote sub-4.5 captions to --text-2. Re-check the amber badge text against both surfaces.

**Accessibility-6. Add a scale legend and non-color cue to the Explorer heatmap**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · dataviz_

Column identity is a 6px color dot + a truncated mono label. In grayscale/colorblind mode adjacent model columns collapse to near-identical shades. The numeric token count printed in each cell is the only non-color signal, and it's absent for zero cells.
- Fix: Add a compact color-scale legend near the heatmap header ('fewer → more tokens' with the actual min/max token values), and ensure the per-cell number is always the primary read. **Accessibility-7. Make the heatmap grid a proper keyboard grid, not hundreds of tab stops**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · interaction_
- Problem: ServiceModelHeatmap.tsx gives every cell role=button + tabIndex=0 (up to 30 rows × N model columns), so a keyboard user must Tab through potentially hundreds of stops — including zero-token cells that open a 'No data' modal. The container uses role=table but without rowgroup/row/columnheader/rowheader roles, so assistive tech gets a table shell with button cells and no header association.

## Cross-cutting: empty / loading / error / no-data states

_The app has a strong, purpose-built vocabulary for missing-telemetry states (EmptyState, ErrorState/ErrorBanner, DataGapNote, MissingDataHint, ExampleDataFrame, CapabilityGate) and a few exemplary usages (SessionUserCostPanel, PromptsTable's EvaluationsEmptyState, AgentContextStoresSubview, AttributeAuditPage). But these strong patterns are applied inconsistently: most self-fetching panels fall back to a single muted line of text ("No X in the current scope."), capability-gated panels vanish to null, and per-section query errors are silently rendered as "no data." Because this tenant frequently lacks instrumentation, the biggest weakness is that empty/partial states rarely distinguish "no activity" from "attribute not emitted" or "scan budget hit," and rarely tell the user the concrete next step even though the building blocks to do so already exist._

**Cross-cutting:-1. ⭐ Stop capability-gated panels from vanishing to null**  ·  _high impact · M effort · empty-states_
- Problem: CapabilityGate returns null both while the probe loads AND when the capability is absent, so SafetyPanel, FeedbackPanel (Pulse), RagPanel (Explorer), and CacheCostPanel (Models FinOps) disappear entirely on tenants that don't emit the attribute. The user never learns the feature exists or what to instrument — the exact opposite of the app's stated 'keep the UI honest' goal in DataGapNote. AgentContextStoresSubview already proves the right pattern by using cap.status() to render an EmptyState instead of nothing.
- Fix: When a capability is definitively absent (cap.status()==='absent', not 'loading'/'unknown'), render a collapsed 'available with instrumentation' affordance via EmptyState/DataGapNote naming the gating attribute (e.g. gen_ai.feedback.* for FeedbackPanel, db.system/vector_db.* for RagPanel) and the fix, instead of returning null. Reserve null strictly for the loading phase. Reuse the cap.status() approach already in AgentContextStoresSubview.

**Cross-cutting:-2. ⭐ Surface per-section query errors instead of mislabeling them 'no data'**  ·  _high impact · M effort · empty-states_
- Problem: Self-fetching panel bodies branch only isLoading -> length===0, never checking result.error, even though the hooks expose it (e.g. useProviderMix returns error at ui/app/pages/Pulse/useProviderMix.ts:146 but ProviderMixBody only checks isLoading then slices.length===0). **Cross-cutting:-3. ⭐ Make empty states distinguish 'no activity' from 'missing instrumentation'**  ·  _high impact · L effort · empty-states_
- Problem: About 35 panels render a single muted 12.5px line like 'No X in the current scope.' But only ArchitectureMap mentions scan budget in its empty copy ('...scope / scan budget — widen the timeframe or raise the scan limit.'). ## Cross-cutting: scan-cost, truncation, sampling & data-freshness transparency

_The app routes every query through useScopedDql, which injects a global scan limit (default 500 GB), a sampling ratio, and reports per-query Grail scan telemetry (scannedBytes, executionMs, limitHit) into ScanReportContext. The plumbing is genuinely good, but almost none of it reaches a normal user: the scan pills (PageScanTotal, TileScanFooter) are gated behind showScanDebug which defaults to false, the per-tile scan/limit attribution (ScanScope/TileScanFooter) is wired ONLY on the Summary page, and sampling-extrapolation caveats live exclusively inside hover-only info tooltips. The result is a serious fidelity-trust gap: on Pulse, Models/FinOps, Agents, etc. a KPI that silently hit the 500 GB cap or was extrapolated from a 1-in-100 sample renders as an exact, authoritative number with no visible caveat. The biggest weaknesses are (1) truncation is invisible outside Summary, (2) extrapolation is undisclosed on the numbers themselves, and (3) the scan-limit/sampling controls give no cost-vs-fidelity guidance._

**Cross-cutting:-1. ⭐ Give the scan-limit and sampling selectors a legible cost-vs-fidelity trade-off**  ·  _high impact · S effort · labeling_
- Problem: ScanLimitSegmented is a bare Select of '500 GB / 1 TB / 2 TB / 5 TB' with only aria-label='Scan limit' and no helper text — nothing tells the user that higher = more complete but slower/costlier, nor that 500 GB (the default) routinely truncates busy tenants (the design spec itself measured 'fetch spans' over 1h hitting the 500 GB cap). SamplingSegmented similarly lists 'None / 10 / 100 / 1k / 10k' with no indication that raising it trades fidelity for speed/cost. Users are setting the single most important cost/fidelity levers blind.
- Fix: Add a short helper line or info tooltip under each LabeledField in GlobalFilterStrip.tsx: for Scan limit, 'Higher = fewer truncated results, but slower & more scan cost. 500 GB may truncate high-volume tenants.'; for Sampling, 'Higher ratio = faster/cheaper, but counts become extrapolated estimates.' Optionally annotate options (e.g. '500 GB · fastest', '5 TB · most complete') so the trade-off is visible at the point of choice.

**Cross-cutting:-2. ⭐ Surface truncation ('limit hit') app-wide, independent of the debug toggle**  ·  _high impact · M effort · insight_
- Problem: readScanMeta already computes limitHit (scannedBytes >= 98% of budget, or a Grail LIMIT/SCAN notification), but the ONLY always-on warning that consumes it is TileScanFooter.tsx's 'Scan limit reached — values may be partial.' — and ScanScope/TileScanFooter is wired only under pages/Summary/* (grep shows PostureBand/CustomizableGrid/SummaryCard/SummaryPage). On Pulse, Models/FinOps, Agents, Prompts, Explorer a query that hit the 500 GB default cap and under-counted a sum()/count() shows as a hard, authoritative number with zero caveat. The other consumer, PageScanTotal's ' ⚠', is gated behind showScanDebug which defaults false (TweaksContext.tsx:141).
- Fix: Decouple the limitHit signal from showScanDebug and from the Summary-only footer. Add a lightweight always-on page-level 'partial data' indicator in ResolutionStatusLine (e.g. an amber chip 'Some results truncated at the 500 GB scan limit — raise it or narrow scope' derived from useScanTotal().limitHit), and render the existing 'values may be partial' micro-note on truncated tiles on every page by finishing the app-wide ScanScopedTile adoption the spec's Deliverable 2 planned but only shipped on Summary.

**Cross-cutting:-3. ⭐ Disclose sampling extrapolation on the numbers themselves, not just in tooltips**  ·  _high impact · M effort · labeling_
- Problem: When sampling is on (e.g. 1-in-100), extrapolate()/extrapolateSeries multiply count/sum aggregates by the ratio to reconstruct the population (SamplingContext.tsx; call sites in usePulseSummary, useHiddenFailures, useFinOps, etc.). But the only disclosure is prose buried inside info= tooltips a user must hover (SummaryTilesRow.tsx:819,838). A headline like total tokens or USD spend rendered from a 1-in-10000 sample looks exact to ±0, yet carries large sampling error. The toolbar 'Sampling' Select (SamplingSegmented.tsx) also gives no warning that raising it turns every headline count into an estimate.
- Fix: When samplingRatio > 1, render a persistent affordance on extrapolated count/sum values — a leading '≈' or a small 'est.' badge (reuse the existing EstimatedBadge pattern in DetailModal.tsx) — and add a toolbar-level note next to the Sampling selector such as 'Counts/spend extrapolated ×100 — estimate'. Leave sampling-invariant stats (percentiles, rates, averages) unbadged, matching the code's own extrapolate/don't-extrapolate split.

**Cross-cutting:-4. ⭐ Promote a minimal always-on scan-cost readout instead of a dev-only neon pill**  ·  _high impact · M effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: All real scan-cost feedback (PageScanTotal in ScanDebug.tsx, per-tile pills) is gated behind showScanDebug=false and styled as an engineer diagnostic — a neon-mint monospace chip reading 'page · 7 queries · 1.2 TB · 3.4s ⚠'. A normal user driving a 500 GB-limited app that can scan TBs per page never learns what a page costs or how close it ran to its budget, so they can't reason about cost or fidelity at all.
- Fix: Keep the verbose neon pill for debug mode, but add a quiet always-on cost/fidelity summary to ResolutionStatusLine (next to 'Last refreshed') using useScanTotal(): e.g. 'Scanned 1.2 TB · 2 queries truncated'. Phrase it for users, use Strato text tokens, and make the truncated count a click target that opens the scan-limit selector.

**Cross-cutting:-5. Use a semantic warning color for truncation, not the neon-mint diagnostic pill**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · a11y_
- Problem: PageScanTotal renders truncation as ' ⚠' appended in the same electric-mint (#39ffb0 on #0b1220) chip used for normal scan stats (ScanDebug.tsx neonPill), so a genuine data-fidelity alert reads as neutral diagnostic decoration. neonPill also hardcodes its own colors 'so it reads the same in light and dark', bypassing Strato theme tokens, at 9.5–11px mono — small and off-system. The truncation state is semantically warning/danger and should look like it.
- Fix: Split truncation out of the neon pill into a Strato-tokened warning treatment (amber/critical color, warning icon) that respects light/dark themes, matching the amber 'Scan limit reached' text already used in TileScanFooter.tsx (var(--amber)). Reserve the neon chip for the neutral bytes/time diagnostic. Bump the minimum font size for legibility.

**Cross-cutting:-6. Disclose per-tile sampling overrides that differ from the toolbar setting**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · consistency_
- Problem: Heavy background estimates force their own sampling via samplingRatioOverride (useScopedDql.ts:68) — e.g. useDailySpend.ts and useFinOps.ts run at a coarser ratio and extrapolate, independent of the toolbar. A user who set Sampling to 'None' can be looking at a spend/run-rate tile secretly computed at 1-in-1000, with no indication that this specific tile is a coarser estimate than the global control implies. That is a silent, per-tile fidelity inconsistency in exactly the money-sensitive FinOps numbers.
- Fix: When a tile's effective sampling ratio differs from the toolbar ratio, mark it (e.g. an 'est. (sampled 1-in-1000)' sub-label or the same '≈' badge from suggestion 2) so the override is visible. **Cross-cutting:-7. Show scanned-vs-budget ratio so users can judge truncation severity**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · insight_
- Problem: limitHit is a single boolean (ScanReportContext.tsx:202) — a query that scanned 500/500 GB and one that would have needed 5 TB both surface identically as 'partial'. The heuristic (>=98% of budget) can also false-positive on a query that legitimately scans near its budget without truncating. - Problem: ResolutionStatusLine.tsx shows a single global 'Last refreshed 3m ago' from one hook (useResolvedCounts) that never changes color or warns as it ages, and it reflects the counts query, not the slowest/oldest data on the page. Meanwhile react-query staleTime (e.g. 60_000 in CapabilityContext.tsx) can serve cached results older than the label implies, and heavy background estimates refresh on their own cadence — so 'refreshed 10s ago' can sit above minutes-old cached tiles.

## Information architecture & visual hierarchy

_The app exposes eight visually-identical top-nav tabs, but two of them (Summary and Pulse) are overlapping overview dashboards — both call usePulseSummary(), Summary is the "/" landing and re-imports Pulse's cards, and Pulse leads with the architecture map plus its own 9-tile row — so a first-time user has two competing "front doors" and no clear source of truth. Hierarchy is weakened by six of eight pages having no in-page title, seven bespoke-but-near-identical KPI tile-row components, and a long repeated DataGapNote paragraph pushing real data down on almost every page. Utility surfaces (Attributes audit, About) and dead boilerplate routes (/home, unrouted Data.tsx) sit at the same weight as the core analytics tabs, and there is no app-level first-run/empty state for a tenant with no AI telemetry._

**Information-1. ⭐ Group and demote the 8 flat tabs; move About/Attributes out of the primary row**  ·  _high impact · M effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: NAV_ITEMS renders 8 equal-weight pills: Summary, Pulse, Explorer, Agents, Prompts, Models / FinOps, Attributes, About. There is no grouping between overview (Summary/Pulse), entity analytics (Explorer/Agents/Prompts/Models), and meta/diagnostic surfaces (Attributes audit, About). 'About' is a low-frequency attribution page and 'Attributes' is an instrumentation-coverage diagnostic — both compete for the same attention as core analytics, raising cognitive load and pushing the nav toward crowding when combined with the logo, timeframe, pricing, and Tweaks action items.
- Fix: Reduce the primary row to the ~5-6 analytical tabs. Move 'About' into an overflow/settings menu or footer link, and place 'Attributes' (the audit) under a secondary/utility affordance or a divider. Add a subtle visual separator or section grouping in AppHeader.Navigation so overview vs entities read as distinct clusters.

**Information-2. ⭐ Add a consistent page title + one-line purpose to every page**  ·  _high impact · M effort · consistency_
- Problem: Only AttributeAuditPage ('AI Attribute Audit') and About render a Heading level={1}. Summary, Pulse, Explorer, Agents, Models, and Prompts render no page title or description at all — the only 'you are here' cue is the active nav pill. On deep-link or tab switch there is no in-page identity, no statement of what the page answers, and no page-level scope/timeframe echo, so orientation depends entirely on the tab bar and the dense GlobalFilterStrip.
- Fix: Introduce a shared PageHeader (title + short purpose line + optional timeframe/tenant echo) and render it at the top of all eight pages, reusing AttributeAudit's pattern (icon + Heading level=1 + subtitle). E.g. Agents → 'Agents · per-agent latency, cost, and orchestration health'. This also fixes the a11y gap of pages lacking an h1 landmark.

**Information-3. ⭐ Resolve the two-front-door overlap between Summary and Pulse**  ·  _high impact · L effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: Summary (index route '/' and '/summary') and Pulse (2nd tab) are both overview dashboards driven by the same usePulseSummary() hook; SummaryPage even imports QualityTrustCard/FinOpsCard/LatencyTierCard etc. from ../Pulse, and its own comment concedes 'It does NOT replace Pulse'. A first-time user lands on Summary's fleet-grade + 6 KPIs, clicks Pulse, and sees a different 9-tile framing (Tokens/Spend/P95/Error/Models/MCP/Tools/Cost/Efficiency/Findings) plus the architecture map — same domain, different tiles, no stated relationship.
- Fix: Give each a distinct job and make it explicit. Recommended: keep Summary as the single exec 'AI health at a glance' landing and reframe Pulse as 'Live topology & flow' (lead with the ArchitectureMap, drop the redundant KPI tiles that Summary already owns, or collapse them into a one-line strip). Add a subtitle to each page stating its role ('Executive fleet posture' vs 'Live architecture & signal flow'). If they can't be differentiated, merge Pulse's map into Summary and reclaim a tab.

**Information-4. ⭐ Consolidate the 7 bespoke KPI tile-row components into one shared KpiRow**  ·  _high impact · L effort · consistency_
- Problem: There are at least seven near-identical KPI-tile implementations: PostureBand's KpiTile (value 20px), Pulse SummaryTilesRow's Tile (value 22px), AttributeAudit's HeroStat (22px), plus AgentsTilesRow, ModelsTilesRow, PromptsTilesRow, ExplorerTiles, FinOpsTilesRow. They diverge in font size, label casing, delta/sparkline treatment, and loading skeletons, so the 'most important number' reads differently on every page and duplicated code drifts over time.
- Fix: Extract a single KpiTile + KpiRow primitive (label, value, delta, spark, tone, loading) and refactor all page tile-rows onto it, standardizing value type scale, uppercase-label style, and skeletons. This unifies the numeric hierarchy across pages and removes ~6 redundant implementations.

**Information-5. Delete dead boilerplate routes and folded-page leftovers**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · consistency_
- Problem: App.tsx still routes '/home' to Home.tsx, which is the untouched starter template ('Welcome To Your Dynatrace App — Edit ui/app/pages/Home.tsx'). Data.tsx is the starter DQL-runner and is imported/routed nowhere. The folded tabs (Tools/Topology/McpHealth/FinOps) correctly redirect, but their page directories (pages/Tools, pages/Topology, pages/McpHealth) still linger. This dead surface misrepresents the true IA to maintainers and risks an accidental link to boilerplate.
- Fix: Remove the /home route and Home.tsx, delete unrouted Data.tsx, and prune the folded page directories that survive only via RedirectKeepingSearch. Keep the redirects themselves so old deep-links still resolve.

**Information-6. Fix the stale nav documentation and the one inconsistent tab label**  ·  _medium impact · S effort · labeling_
- Problem: Header.tsx's NAV_ITEMS comment states 'Five-tab structure (the redesign): Tools + Topology fold into Agents...' but the array actually defines eight tabs — the mental model in the code is two revisions behind, a maintenance hazard. Separately, 'Models / FinOps' is the only compound/slashed label among otherwise single-word tabs, an inconsistency in the nav's labeling rhythm.
- Fix: Update the Header comment to describe the real 8-tab (or reduced) structure, and normalize the label to a single word ('Models', with FinOps as an in-page section/subtitle) so tab labels stay parallel.

**Information-7. Add an app-level first-run / no-AI-data state**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · empty-states_
- Problem: Only AttributeAuditPage has a real EmptyState ('No AI spans found in this window'). On Summary/Pulse a tenant with no AI telemetry sees the fleet grade fall back to '—', empty donuts, and the footprint string 'No AI activity in the current scope.' scattered across many independent cards, rather than one clear message. A first-time user can't tell 'nothing is instrumented yet' from 'my filters are too narrow'.
- Fix: Detect the zero-AI-spans condition once (reuse the audit's isEmpty signal) and render a single app-level empty/onboarding panel on the landing page: 'No AI activity detected — check instrumentation or widen the timeframe', with links to the Attributes audit and the timeframe control. Suppress the wall of per-card '—' tiles in that state.

**Information-8. Condense and de-duplicate the per-page DataGapNotes so they stop outranking the data**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: Pulse, Agents (x3), Explorer, Models, and Prompts each render a long DataGapNote paragraph high on the page, repeating the same instrumentation asks (TTFT, session.id, gen_ai.agent.name, trace-context propagation). These multi-sentence blocks sit above or beside the KPIs, so on several pages the first thing the eye lands on is a caveat, not the metric it qualifies.
- Fix: Collapse DataGapNote to a compact, dismissible one-liner with a chevron ('Some signals are unavailable — why?') that expands to the detail, and centralize the full instrumentation checklist on the Attributes page (link to it). Consider showing each note inline next to the specific metric it degrades rather than as a page-top banner.

**Information-9. Give Pulse a single top-line health headline**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: Pulse leads with the ArchitectureMap plus a 9-tile SummaryTilesRow where every tile (Tokens, Spend, P95, Error rate, Models, MCP servers/tools, Cost/req, Token efficiency, Avg tokens, Active findings) has equal visual weight and there is no single 'is the platform healthy right now' number. An SRE scanning Pulse has to read ten tiles and a diagram to form a judgment, unlike Summary which has a clear trust grade.
- Fix: Add one prominent status headline to the Pulse hero (e.g. reuse PlatformHealthCard's score or the fleet grade) above/beside the map, and visually rank the 9 tiles (promote Error rate + Active findings, demote Avg tokens/req) so the operational signal reads first.

**Information-10. Rebalance the GlobalFilterStrip so power-user controls don't sit at equal weight**  ·  _medium impact · M effort · hierarchy_
- Problem: The persistent GlobalFilterStrip under the nav exposes Segments, Sampling, Scan limit, Filters, Reload, and Reset at equal prominence, plus a ResolutionStatusLine row beneath. Sampling and Scan-limit are power-user Grail-cost concepts, yet they get the same label treatment and space as Segments for every user; combined with per-page focus chips and DataGapNotes, a lot of vertical space precedes the first real content on each page.
- Fix: Promote Segments + Filters as the primary scope controls and tuck Sampling/Scan-limit behind a compact 'Advanced' popover or a single settings affordance. Consider making the strip collapse to a one-line summary of the active scope once set, expanding on demand, to reclaim above-the-fold space.
